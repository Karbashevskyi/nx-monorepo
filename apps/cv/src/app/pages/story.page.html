My story

From a city budget portal to my own studio

The CV lists what I did. This page tells how I got here: thirteen years of building products, mostly on my own, from Chernivtsi to Warsaw.

  1. Early years

    Chernivtsi · School

    Ideas ahead of resources

    Long before my first paid project I was already trying to build my own products while still at school. One of them was Falcon, my own online marketplace, similar in idea to Prom.ua, which was built by a company from Kyiv.

    The same ideas often come to many people at once; the real question is who gets to finish them. Teams in Kyiv were older, there were more of them, they had money and did not have to go to school. I had no connections, no money and lessons every morning. That taught me to do everything myself and to see things through.

    I tried hardware too: an IoT ticket vending machine for public transport, assembled from whatever parts I could find. It almost worked, but after talking to people at the final stops I realised everyone was fine with things as they were, and a single city ticket still does not exist today. It was my first lesson in talking to users before building.

  2. 2013

    Chernivtsi · Age 17

    First commercial projects

    I am self-taught. I took my first paid projects at 17, in my home city of Chernivtsi. From the very beginning I did the whole job myself: listened to what the client needed, picked the technology, designed the interface, wrote the code and launched it.

    That habit of owning a product end to end has stayed with me ever since: more than 70% of my products were built single-handedly.

  3. 2014

    Chernivtsi · Student

    Open Budget of Chernivtsi

    In 2014, still a student, not yet 18 and without any formal IT education, I started building the Open Budget of Chernivtsi (cv.ofd.in.ua), Ukraine's first municipal budget visualization system. The initiative came from the then mayor of Chernivtsi, Oleksiy Kaspruk, and his first deputy; I built it on my own, in my own time and at my own expense, for free. At the end of 2014 we launched the first, still rough version, long before then-President Petro Poroshenko even signed the law "On the openness of the use of public funds". Chernivtsi became the first city in Ukraine to officially adopt such a platform.

    The hardest part was the data. Every department, school and hospital reported in its own way, in Word files and ad-hoc tables. I wrote an algorithm in vanilla PHP (I did not know the Yii2 framework well yet) that adapts to any Excel table layout, recognises budget classification codes and consolidates hundreds of reports into one database. Then I proposed moving all budget holders to a single standard and designed its base template from the data already collected.

    For the first time, residents could see every item of the city's revenue and expenditure as interactive charts and tables and download the original reports.

  4. 2015 – 2017

    Chernivtsi · TsNAP

    TsNAP and the second version of the portal

    After I graduated from the Chernivtsi Higher Vocational School of Radio Electronics, the city council offered me a job as system administrator at the Administrative Services Centre (TsNAP), which was just opening. I believe the reason was the budget portal: a complex system I had built for free, on my own and without a specialised education.

    At TsNAP I was asked to build the second version of the budget portal, this time on the Yii2 framework, and it is still running today. So that the city could pay me monthly for its use and maintenance, I founded my own company, Hashlink.

    I also built an application for the centre's administrators that sped up the reception of residents, and proposed and created an online status check, so people no longer had to come in person to learn how their application for an administrative service was progressing.

    Finally, I turned an old, non-working information kiosk into a complete system: visitors got the information they needed at the kiosk or on the website at home, often without even taking a queue ticket to an administrator.

  5. 2017 – 2018

    Warsaw

    Poland and the European tech sector

    In 2017 I started my engineering degree in Computer Science at AEH in Warsaw, and in 2018 I joined EFENDI as a front-end developer.

    At EFENDI I built mobile apps and web portals for international clients such as Siemens, Samsung and Volkswagen, working with Ionic, Angular, Flutter and Firebase, and set up CI/CD and Docker-based environments.

  6. 2018 – 2021

    Side projects

    Salo-Sale and Thiis

    In parallel I founded Salo-Sale: a discount platform for local businesses with an admin panel, a business panel, two Flutter mobile apps and an SEO-optimised website. It taught me how to run a product and a team, especially through the COVID-19 period.

    In 2021 I published Thiis, an open-source JavaScript library that makes data validation short and readable, and since 2023 have been writing about it, Angular and async patterns on dev.to and Medium.

  7. 2021 – today

    UNICARD Systems

    From developer to tech lead

    I joined UNICARD Systems as a frontend developer and was soon promoted to frontend tech lead. I trained the team, introduced new ways of planning (half as many unfinished tasks, ~40% less time on sprint planning), upgraded Angular to make builds twice as fast and shipped a PWA that brought more positive customer feedback and sales.

    In 2023 I led the frontend of a project at SKK, moving a .NET application to Angular from scratch and delivering it on time. Along the way I completed my Master of Engineering in Computer Science at AEH (2022–2024), with a thesis graded 5.0.

  8. 2022 – today

    Car4Ukraine

    Technology for a cause

    When the full-scale war began, I co-created Car4Ukraine with people from IT, logistics, media and education. We source, upgrade and deliver vehicles to Ukrainian defenders: 850+ vehicles so far, with 250+ volunteers from 30+ countries.

    I build and maintain the car4ukraine.com platform: a multilingual website with fundraising campaigns, real-time progress and a car-donation flow.

  9. 2025 – today

    Founder

    amator.app and karba.studio

    With my co-founder I am building amator.app, a sport-tech platform that helps amateur players find matches and teammates in Warsaw, Katowice and Larnaca. I built the whole product: Angular, Supabase, real-time WebSockets and iOS/Android apps with Capacitor.

    I am also launching karba.studio, a digital studio for websites, mobile apps, UI/UX design, marketing and SEO, to bring this end-to-end approach to more clients with a team of strong specialists.

Today

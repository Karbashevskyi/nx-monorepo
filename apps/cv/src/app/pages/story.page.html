My story

From a city budget portal to my own studio

The CV lists what I did. This page tells how I got here: thirteen years of building products, mostly on my own, from Chernivtsi to Warsaw.

  1. 2013

    Chernivtsi · Age 17

    First commercial projects

    I took my first paid projects at 17, in my home city of Chernivtsi. From the very beginning I did the whole job myself: listened to what the client needed, picked the technology, designed the interface, wrote the code and launched it.

    That habit of owning a product end to end has stayed with me ever since: more than 70% of my products were built single-handedly.

  2. 2015

    Chernivtsi · Age 19

    Open Budget of Chernivtsi

    While studying and working as a computer systems specialist at Chernivtsi City Council, I designed and built cv.ofd.in.ua, Ukraine's first municipal budget visualization system. Chernivtsi became the first city in Ukraine to officially adopt such a platform, before budget transparency became mandatory nationwide.

    The hardest part was not the code. Every department, school and hospital reported in its own Word files, so I proposed one machine-readable Excel standard for all budget holders and wrote parsers that turned hundreds of reports into one database and clear interactive charts for residents. I released it under my own brand, Hashlink.

  3. 2017 – 2018

    Warsaw

    Poland and the European tech sector

    In 2017 I started my engineering degree in Computer Science at AEH in Warsaw, and in 2018 I joined EFENDI as a front-end developer.

    At EFENDI I built mobile apps and web portals for international clients such as Siemens, Samsung and Volkswagen, working with Ionic, Angular, Flutter and Firebase, and set up CI/CD and Docker-based environments.

  4. 2018 – 2021

    Side projects

    Salo-Sale and Thiis

    In parallel I founded Salo-Sale: a discount platform for local businesses with an admin panel, a business panel, two Flutter mobile apps and an SEO-optimised website. It taught me how to run a product and a team, especially through the COVID-19 period.

    In 2021 I published Thiis, an open-source JavaScript library that makes data validation short and readable, and wrote about it on Medium and dev.to.

  5. 2021 – today

    UNICARD Systems

    From developer to tech lead

    I joined UNICARD Systems as a frontend developer and was soon promoted to frontend tech lead. I trained the team, introduced new ways of planning (half as many unfinished tasks, ~40% less time on sprint planning), upgraded Angular to make builds twice as fast and shipped a PWA that brought more positive customer feedback and sales.

    In 2023 I led the frontend of a project at SKK, moving a .NET application to Angular from scratch and delivering it on time. Along the way I completed my Master of Engineering in Computer Science at AEH (2022–2024), with a thesis graded 5.0.

  6. 2022 – today

    Car4Ukraine

    Technology for a cause

    When the full-scale war began, I co-created Car4Ukraine with people from IT, logistics, media and education. We source, upgrade and deliver vehicles to Ukrainian defenders: 850+ vehicles so far, with 250+ volunteers from 30+ countries.

    I build and maintain the car4ukraine.com platform: a multilingual website with fundraising campaigns, real-time progress and a car-donation flow.

  7. 2025 – today

    Founder

    amator.app and karba.studio

    With my co-founder I am building amator.app, a sport-tech platform that helps amateur players find matches and teammates in Warsaw, Katowice and Larnaca. I built the whole product: Angular, Supabase, real-time WebSockets and iOS/Android apps with Capacitor.

    I am also launching karba.studio, a digital studio for websites, mobile apps, UI/UX design, marketing and SEO, to bring this end-to-end approach to more clients with a team of strong specialists.

Today

Whatever the project, I start by understanding the problem and the people behind it, then choose the technology that fits, build the product end to end and leave documentation good enough to hand it over to a team. If that is the kind of person you are looking for, let's talk.
